# Relationship Pattern Catalog

This is the pattern vocabulary the future AI layer should learn to recognize. A pattern is only reportable when repeated behavior or a clear time-series change supports it.

## Cross-relationship patterns

- **Initiative asymmetry** — one person repeatedly reopens the conversation.
- **Response-effort asymmetry** — one person answers but rarely expands, asks back or adds new material.
- **Speed vs effort split** — fast replies with low content effort, or slow replies with high effort.
- **Burst / silence cycle** — intense communication followed by repeated long gaps.
- **Re-entry pattern** — how the conversation resumes after silence and who usually reopens it.
- **Then-vs-now shift** — visible change in initiation, response rhythm, volume, warmth or planning.
- **Question asymmetry** — one person repeatedly shows more conversational curiosity.
- **Topic avoidance** — recurring subjects that are acknowledged but not answered directly.
- **Repair loop** — friction followed by apology, clarification, humor, reassurance or practical repair.
- **Unresolved loop** — the same conflict/topic returns without a visible resolution.
- **Planning loop** — plans are proposed, accepted, delayed, canceled or completed in a recurring pattern.
- **Words/actions mismatch** — positive language without observable follow-through, or the reverse.
- **Boundary pattern** — boundary requests and whether later behavior respects them.
- **Private language** — recurring nicknames, jokes, phrases or shorthand unique to the relationship.
- **Logistics load** — one person carries scheduling, reminders, coordination or practical work.

## Dating / relationship

Prioritize:

- interest signals
- effort
- consistency
- follow-through
- mixed signals
- repair and boundaries

Important distinction: **interest is not effort**. Affectionate language can coexist with weak consistency or follow-through.

## Ex / former relationship

Prioritize:

- who re-engages
- push/pull cycles
- boundary clarity
- nostalgia vs practical reconnection
- unresolved topics
- closure attempts
- changes after long silence

Do not equate renewed contact with wanting to reunite.

## Friends

Prioritize:

- check-ins without a practical reason
- plan initiation and follow-through
- shared humor/private language
- mutual support
- reliability during important moments
- repair after distance or conflict

## Family

Prioritize:

- care/check-ins
- practical support
- responsibility distribution
- family logistics
- conflict and repair style
- boundaries
- who acts as connector/mediator

## Work

Prioritize:

- responsiveness
- clarity of requests
- ownership and task closure
- initiative
- decision follow-through
- escalation/friction
- ambiguity and repeated rework

Avoid emotional interpretations that are not relevant to the work context.

## Other

Require a user-written description of the relationship. Start with the cross-relationship patterns, then adapt the lens using that description and the user's question.

## Evidence standard

A confident pattern should normally have at least one of:

- repeated examples across different dates;
- a measurable time-series change;
- a clear sequence (request → response → follow-through / no follow-through);
- multiple independent behavioral signals pointing the same way.

One dramatic message should be treated as a moment, not a pattern.
